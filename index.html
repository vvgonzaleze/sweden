Etymology:

The nameSvìar (Swedes) comes from a Proto-Germanic root meaning “our own people.” Roman authors like Tacitus already mentioned them in the 1st century CE as a powerful seafaring group, long before the Viking Age. During the Viking Age, the Swedes were closely linked to eastern trade routes, which helped spread their name across Europe. The term Svíaríki combined the ideas of people and power, and over time it evolved into Sverige. The name Sweden therefore reflects both shared identity and political authority.

 Sweden History:

Sweden’s history begins after the Ice Age, with early farming societies and the formation of the Swedes as a distinct people. During the Viking Age, Swedes were mainly involved in eastern trade. In the Middle Ages, Sweden became a Christian kingdom and was later ruled by Denmark within the Kalmar Union until gaining independence in 1523 under Gustav Vasa. Sweden became a major European power in the 17th century but declined after military defeats in the early 18th century. In the 19th and 20th centuries, it industrialized, democratized, and avoided major wars. Today, Sweden is a neutral, democratic country known for stability, welfare, and high living standards.
