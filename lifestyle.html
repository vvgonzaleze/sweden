Work–life balance

Shorter working hours than many countries

At least 25 paid vacation days by law

Strong parental leave (for both parents)

Overtime culture is minimal — leaving work on time is normal
Healthcare & education

Universal healthcare: high quality, low cost (though wait times can happen for non-urgent care)

Education is free, including universities for EU citizens

Very strong focus on child welfare and education quality
Income & cost of living

Salaries are generally good, and income inequality is low

Cost of living is high, especially housing, food, and eating out

Taxes are high, but they pay for healthcare, education, transit, childcare, etc.

 
Environment & lifestyle

Super clean cities, lots of green space

Easy access to nature — forests, lakes, and coastlines

Strong environmental awareness and sustainability

Excellent public transport; many people bike year-round (yes, even in winter)
The downsides


Long, dark winters can be tough mentally

Making close friends can take time, especially as a newcomer

Housing shortages in big cities like Stockholm and Gothenburg

Bureaucracy can be slow and very rule-focused
